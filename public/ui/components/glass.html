    Glass

    See-through surfaces in the "liquid glass" style: a see-through sheet with light inside its edge and a soft shadow, and lighter panels inside it. In Chrome and Edge the small script oc-glass-lens.js turns it into a real lens: the backdrop stays sharp and bends along the edge; other browsers show frosted glass. For a widget or a floating panel over a photo or a gradient, and for a desktop window (Electron, Tauri) with Windows acrylic or macOS vibrancy behind a transparent page. Cards inside a sheet turn into glass panels by themselves. With "reduce transparency" switched on, the surfaces turn solid. All components.

    Lens over a landscape v0.17.0

    Tip: drag the glass card with your mouse or finger (or focus it and use the arrow keys) to see the effect. With oc-glass-lens.js on the page, Chrome and Edge show real liquid glass: the backdrop stays sharp in the middle and bends along the edge, as through a thick piece of glass. Drag the card over the landscape (with the mouse, a finger, or focus it and use the arrow keys) and watch the horizon and the sun bend at its edge. Firefox and Safari show frosted glass instead. See Lens for how it works.

        $items = [];

            }
            $items[] = ['label' => $label, 'icon' => $icon, 'href' => '?tool='.$key, 'current' => $this->tool === $key];
        }

        return $items;
    }

    public function render()
    {
        return view('livewire.media-bars');
    }
}

    Lens

    v0.17.0

    Liquid glass is five things: a see-through fill, light inside the edge, a map, one filter and a fallback.

    1. Fill: a very light white tint (--oc-glass-sheet, 12 %) and a thin white border (35 %), radius 28px.
    2. Thickness: only inset shadows: a bright line just inside the top edge, a weaker one along the bottom and a soft glow all round. No gradient, no rim.
    3. Map: an image the exact size of the sheet. Red runs from left to right, blue from top to bottom; a blurred purple rectangle (half red, half blue: no shift) covers the middle, so only the edge moves.
    4. Filter: an SVG filter reads the map (feImage) and shifts the backdrop with it (feDisplacementMap, scale -80: a bigger number bends more, the minus sign turns the direction); backdrop-filter: url(#…) blur(2px) saturate(160%) applies it.
    5. Fallback: only Chromium (Chrome, Edge, Opera, Electron) applies an SVG filter in backdrop-filter. Elsewhere, and without the script, the sheet is frosted glass: blur(12px) saturate(160%). With "reduce transparency" it is solid.

    oc-glass-lens.js does the map and the filter for you: one hidden <svg> with one filter per sheet size (shared, and removed when no sheet uses it), kept in step with a ResizeObserver. It sets --oc-glass-lens and data-oc-lens on the sheet; outside Chromium it only adds html.oc-no-lens. It skips --flat and data-oc-glass-lens="off". The same script makes a sheet with data-oc-glass-drag draggable over its backdrop, in every browser: mouse, pen and touch (no page scroll under the finger), and the keyboard (the sheet takes focus, arrow keys move it 16px, Shift 64px, Home puts it back). It stays inside its parent, or the closest data-oc-glass-drag-area; it moves with translate, so the lens filter stays the same, and with reduced motion the keyboard steps do not glide. Links and buttons inside keep their clicks. The React and Vue wrappers load it themselves (lens, default on); in plain HTML and Blade put the script on the page once.

    Pitfall: in Chrome an outer shadow on the element that carries the lens filter shifts the map, and the glass breaks. Shadows on the glass layer itself are always inset. In .oc-glass the filter sits on a ::after layer inside the sheet, so the sheet's own drop shadow is safe; on your own element, put a drop shadow on a wrapper. OmniaUI Pro's Glass navigation block keeps its own lens (.oc-glass--lens, a wavy water filter); the free script leaves those sheets alone.

    Reference

    Styles live in packages/ui/src/parts/media-glass.css.

    Class / attributeDescription
    .oc-glassThe sheet: a see-through white fill, a thin white border, light inside the edge (inset shadows only) and a soft drop shadow. Frosted glass by default; a lens in Chromium with oc-glass-lens.js. Put it over colour and shapes, as in the demos. .oc-card inside becomes a glass panel. Fill, light and filter sit on a ::after layer behind the content, so a tooltip inside still appears next to its anchor and the drop shadow does not break the lens.
    oc-glass-lens.jsThe lens for .oc-glass, .oc-glass--liquid and .oc-glass-bar (and any [data-oc-glass-lens]): one SVG filter per sheet size, --oc-glass-lens + data-oc-lens on the sheet. Exports initGlassLens, bindGlassLens, supportsGlassLens, lensMap.
    data-oc-glass-drag / data-oc-glass-drag-areaThe sheet can be moved over its backdrop (mouse, touch, arrow keys, Home) / the element it stays inside (default: the parent). Fires oc-glass-move with { x, y }. Exports initGlassDrag, bindGlassDrag, moveGlass.
    data-oc-glass-lens="off" / data-oc-glass-lens-scaleNo lens on this sheet (frosted glass) / a different strength (default -80)
    html.oc-no-lensSet by the script outside Chromium: frosted glass (blur(12px) saturate(160%))
    .oc-glass-panelA lighter layer inside a sheet
    --strongOn the sheet or a panel: more opaque, for text-heavy content
    .oc-glass--flatDesktop window where the OS draws the blur: no own blur, no lens, no drop shadow
    .oc-glass-dividerHairline (<hr>) in the glass color
    .oc-glass--liquidLiquid glass: clear tint, strong blur and saturation, light inside the edge, specular rim
    .oc-glass-bar / __itemFloating menu bar of items (icon + label); the current item (aria-current, aria-pressed, checked radio) sits in a raised bubble
    --icons / --round / __sepIcon-only items / a single round button next to the bar / a divider
    --clear / --frostMore see-through / more frosted glass with a fine grain (on .oc-glass, --liquid and .oc-glass-bar)
    .oc-glass-bar--lightWhite icons and labels for a dark or colourful backdrop
    .oc-glass-stage / --landscapeMoving colour background to show glass on (stops with reduced motion) / a still landscape: sky, sun and hills (night in dark mode)
    --oc-liquid-tint / -edge / -blurTint, edge colour and blur of the liquid glass
    prefers-reduced-transparencySolid surfaces and no blur when the person asks for less transparency
    @supports not (backdrop-filter)Without backdrop-filter the sheet turns nearly solid, so text stays readable
